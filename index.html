01 ▶ Thinking

AI-powered intelligence. Human-made magic.

We’ve spent this year putting AI to work where it helps most – the slow, careful groundwork – so our people have more time for the ideas.

  • Literature triage that surfaces the strongest evidence sooner
  • First-pass reference checks before anything reaches medical review

02 ▶ Congress season

Three things a busy stand taught us

Autumn congresses are loud, crowded and short on time. Here’s what we keep coming back to when we plan a stand.

  1. 1

    One idea per stand

    If the stand needs a second headline, it needs a second stand. We pick the single thought a clinician should leave with and build everything around it.

  2. 2

    Design for the walk-past

    Most people see a stand for a few seconds in an aisle. The headline has to land at walking pace, from the far side of the carpet.

  3. 3

    Give field teams a conversation

    The best stands start dialogue in both directions. We give medical and field teams a prompt to open with, not a brochure to hand over.

04 ▶ Expertise spotlight ▶ Neurology

Making the invisible visible

Neurology asks a lot of an audience. Mechanisms happen at a scale nobody can see, and outcomes unfold over years.

So we start with one honest visual metaphor, built with the medical team and tested with clinicians before it goes anywhere near a layout.

  • Mechanism of disease and mechanism of action films
  • Patient journey mapping with specialist nurses
  • Congress symposia and on-demand education

Navigating the clearest route through complexity.
